    Wiring Harnesses¶

    One Plug Instead of Seven

    Loose jumper wires wiggle out of the breadboard, and one loose wire can cost an hour of debugging. In this guide you will turn a ribbon cable into three harnesses that plug in as blocks and land on the right pins every time. Let's listen closely!

    A wiring harness is a group of wires bundled in a fixed order so they connect as one unit. This kit needs three: one each for the display, the amplifier, and the microphone. Each harness is a strip of Dupont wires torn from a ribbon cable. The module end keeps the ribbon's original color order. At the Pico end, you move only the few wires that must move, so the block plugs into the breadboard beside the Pico with its ground wire on a Pico GND pin.
    Builds Three harnesses: display (8 wires), amplifier (6 wires), microphone (6 wires)
    Time About 30 minutes for all three
    Do it before Lab 04 (display), Lab 05 (amplifier), Lab 06 (microphone)
    Power Unplug the Pico's USB cable while you build and plug in harnesses

    The finished kit. All three harnesses run from the breadboard behind the display. Click any photo to see it full size.

    What You Need¶

    Item Notes
    A 40-wire male-to-female Dupont ribbon cable, 10 cm or 20 cm One ribbon makes all three harnesses for one kit. See Choosing a Wire Length below.
    The display, amplifier, and microphone with their header pins soldered on The female ends of the ribbon push onto these pins.
    Your Pico 2 W on the breadboard Pin 1 in breadboard row 1, as in the circuit diagram.
    Optional: hot glue gun, or narrow tape Holds the male pins together as a block.
    Optional: a small paper tag for each harness Write "DISPLAY", "AMP" or "MIC" on it.

    A Dupont male end has a pin that pushes into a breadboard hole. A female end has a socket that slides over a module's header pin. In every harness, the female ends go on the module and the male ends go in the breadboard.

    Choosing a Wire Length¶

    Both lengths work, and the steps are the same for each. Pick the one that fits how far your parts sit from the breadboard.

    Length Best for Search for
    10 cm Compact builds, where the display, amplifier and microphone sit very close to the breadboard dupont jumper wire 40 pin male to female 10cm
    20 cm Spreading the parts out, such as a display on its own stand or a speaker away from the microphone dupont jumper wire 40 pin male to female 20cm (the ribbon in the Purchasing Guide)

    Where a step says how far to peel wires apart, it gives the 10 cm length first and the 20 cm length in parentheses.

    The Ribbon's Color Order¶

    The wires in a Dupont ribbon follow the resistor color code, and the pattern repeats every ten wires:

    brown, red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, gray, white, black, then brown again.

    Each harness is a strip of neighboring wires taken from this pattern. That is why the colors below are not random: they are simply where each strip falls on the ribbon. In all three harnesses, brown is ground and red is power.

    If your ribbon has a different color order

    Some ribbons use other colors. The method still works: peel a strip of the right length, write down its colors in order, and use your own colors in the tables below. What matters is the order of the wires, not their colors.

    The Big Idea: Every Fifth Row Is Ground¶

    The Pico's left edge has 20 pins, and the breadboard row number equals the Pico's physical pin number: pin 1 is in row 1, pin 20 in row 20. Four of those pins are GND: rows 3, 8, 13 and 18. They fall every fifth row.

    Rows Pico pins Who uses them GND row inside the block
    3 to 10 GND, GP2, GP3, GP4, GP5, GND, GP6, GP7 Display 3 (row 8 stays empty)
    11 GP8 Nobody: a one-row gap
    12 to 16 GP9, GND, GP10, GP11, GP12 Amplifier 13
    17 to 20 GP13, GND, GP14, GP15 Microphone 18

    The kit's pins were chosen so that each module's signal wires sit next to a GND pin. A harness that keeps its wires in the same order as those rows carries its own ground wire, so plugging in the block also connects the module's ground. You never need a separate ground wire.

    Why the Ground Wire Matters Most

    A signal is a voltage measured against ground. If a module's GND wire is missing, its signals have nothing to be measured against, and the module fails in confusing ways instead of with a clear error. A harness that carries its own ground makes that mistake much harder.

    Power is the exception. The 3.3 V pin (3V3, pin 36) and the 5 V pin (VBUS, pin 40) are on the Pico's right edge, so every red wire leaves its harness and crosses to the right side.

    Red wire from Goes to Right-side row
    Display VCC 3V3 (pin 36) 5
    Microphone VDD 3V3 (pin 36) 5
    Amplifier Vin VBUS (pin 40), 5 V from USB 1

    The microphone is a 3.3 V part, so its red wire must go to 3V3, never VBUS. The amplifier works on either. It goes to VBUS here because the Pico covers most holes in each breadboard row, which leaves room for only two wires beside pin 36. If your breadboard has room for three wires in the 3V3 row, the amplifier can use 3V3 instead, as the circuit diagram shows.

    The Three Rules¶

    1. Keep the original color order at the module end. Push the strip's female ends onto the module's pins in order, starting with the wire named in each section.
    2. At the Pico end, move only the wires that must move. Each section lists the moves. Every other wire stays next to its neighbor from the ribbon.
    3. Red goes to the right side. Peel the red wire away from its neighbors along its whole length (about 10 cm of a 20 cm wire), so it can reach across the Pico.

    To peel a strip, nick the gap between two wires with a fingernail and pull the wires apart slowly. The wires separate cleanly along their whole length.

    Harness 1: The Display¶

    The display's header has ten pads: GND, VCC, SCL, SDA, RST, DC, CS, BL, then SDO and TE. Only the first eight get a wire.

    Step 1: Peel an 8-wire strip from brown to gray¶

    Peel a strip of eight wires that starts with brown and ends with gray: brown, red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, gray. These are the display colors used in config.py.

    Step 2: Plug the female ends onto the display¶

    Push the strip onto the display's first eight pads, with brown on GND. Red then lands on VCC, orange on SCL, and so on down to gray on BL. SDO and TE stay bare.

    Step 3: Make two changes at the Pico end¶

    1. Pull red out. Peel the red wire away from brown and orange along its whole length (about 10 cm on 20 cm wires). It goes to 3V3 on the right side.
    2. Split purple and gray off. Peel purple and gray away from blue for about 3 cm (5 cm). Row 8 is a Pico GND pin that the display does not need, so these two wires skip it.

    Step 4: Plug it into the breadboard¶

    Wire Display pin Breadboard row Pico pin
    brown GND 3 GND
    orange SCL 4 GP2
    yellow SDA 5 GP3
    green RST 6 GP4
    blue DC 7 GP5
    (none) 8 GND, left empty
    purple CS 9 GP6
    gray BL 10 GP7
    red VCC 5, right side 3V3 (pin 36)

    The block of five (brown to blue) goes in rows 3 to 7, and the pair (purple and gray) goes in rows 9 and 10. Check it with Lab 04: "Hello World!" should appear inside a cyan ring.

    Harness 2: The Amplifier¶

    The MAX98357A's header has seven pins: LRC, BCLK, DIN, GAIN, SD, GND, Vin. SD (shutdown) is not used.

    Step 1: Peel a 7-wire strip from blue to red¶

    Peel a strip of seven wires that starts with blue and ends with red: blue, purple, gray, white, black, brown, red. This strip crosses the place where the color pattern starts over, which is how brown and red end up on GND and Vin.

    Step 2: Remove the black wire¶

    The black wire lines up with SD. Peel it off the strip completely and set it aside. You now have a strip of four (blue, purple, gray, white) and a pair (brown, red).

    SD Must Never Go to Ground

    Pulling the amplifier's SD pin to ground switches the amplifier off, so a black wire from SD plugged into a GND row gives you a silent speaker and no error. Leave SD with no wire at all; the board's own resistor keeps the amplifier on.

    Step 3: Plug the female ends onto the amplifier¶

    Push the four-wire strip onto LRC, BCLK, DIN and GAIN, with blue on LRC. Leave SD bare. Push the pair onto GND and Vin, with brown on GND and red on Vin.

    Step 4: Make two changes at the Pico end¶

    The amplifier lists LRC before BCLK, but MicroPython needs BCLK on the lower pin, with LRC on the pin right after it. The ground pin sits between them on the Pico.

    1. Swap blue and purple. Peel blue and purple apart for about 3 cm (5 cm) and cross them, so purple (BCLK) comes first.
    2. Put brown between them. Bring the brown GND wire over to sit between purple and blue.

    Red goes to VBUS on the right side.

    Step 5: Plug it into the breadboard¶

    Wire Amplifier pin Breadboard row Pico pin
    purple BCLK 12 GP9
    brown GND 13 GND
    blue LRC 14 GP10
    gray DIN 15 GP11
    white GAIN 16 GP12
    red Vin 1, right side VBUS (pin 40)

    The five wires form one block in rows 12 to 16. Gray and white never move: they are still neighbors from the ribbon. Check it with Lab 05: you should hear a beep, a scale, and three volumes.

    Harness 3: The Microphone¶

    The INMP441 board has two rows of three pins. With the board's label facing you, the top row is SCK, WS, L/R and the bottom row is SD, VDD, GND, with GND under L/R.

    Step 1: Peel a 6-wire strip from gray to orange¶

    Peel a strip of six wires that starts with gray and ends with orange: gray, white, black, brown, red, orange.

    Step 2: Fold the strip around the two pin rows¶

    Split the strip between black and brown for about 2 cm (3 cm), so it can bend into a U.

    1. Push the first three wires onto the top row, left to right: gray on SCK, white on WS, black on L/R.
    2. Fold the strip down around the right edge of the board. The next three wires go onto the bottom row, right to left: brown on GND, red on VDD, orange on SD.

    The order never changes. The strip simply wraps around the board, so brown (GND) sits right under black (L/R).

    Step 3: Make two changes at the Pico end¶

    1. Pull red out. Peel it away along its whole length (about 10 cm on 20 cm wires). It goes to 3V3 on the right side.
    2. Move white past black and brown. On the Pico, the ground pin comes between SCK and WS, so white (WS) moves down one place. Black and brown both go in the ground row, side by side. L/R tied to ground tells the microphone to send the left channel, which is what the kit reads.

    Step 4: Plug it into the breadboard¶

    Wire Microphone pin Breadboard row Pico pin
    gray SCK 17 GP13
    brown GND 18 GND
    black L/R 18 (the next hole out) GND
    white WS 19 GP14
    orange SD 20 GP15
    red VDD 5, right side 3V3 (pin 36)

    Check it with Lab 06: the meter should move when you talk.

    Finishing the Harnesses¶

    Once a harness works, lock its Pico end in place so it plugs in as one block every time:

    • Hot Glue it. Line the male pins up in a spare row of the breadboard with the same spacing they will have beside the Pico, then put a thin bead of hot glue across the plastic housings, not the metal pins. Let it cool before you pull the block out.
    • Or tape it. A narrow wrap of tape around the housings works too, and is easier to undo.
    • Label it. Tape a tag to each harness. A harness with "MIC" on it can't be swapped with the amplifier's by mistake.

    Rear view: each harness ends in a block of black housings glued together, plugged in beside the Pico. The red power wires leave their blocks and cross to the other side.

    Hot Glue the Blocks You Built

    Hot glue the display as two blocks (rows 3 to 7, and rows 9 to 10), the amplifier as one block of five, and the microphone as one block of four plus the black wire. Leave every red wire loose so it can reach the right side.

    Check Before You Power On¶

    Unplug the USB cable, then check each harness against its table:

    1. Each brown wire is in a GND row: 3, 13 or 18.
    2. Each red wire is on the right side: display and microphone in row 5 (3V3), the amplifier in row 1 (VBUS). The microphone's red wire is not in row 1.
    3. Row 8 is empty, and row 11 is empty.
    4. The amplifier's SD pin has no wire.
    5. Every wire is pushed all the way into its hole.

    Side view: the ribbons run from the blocks beside the Pico to the amplifier (center, with the green speaker terminal) and the display (right).

    Then plug in the USB cable and run Lab 02, the hardware probe. It checks the pin plan and the parts it can see, and reports any wiring that breaks the rules.

    Three Harnesses, Zero Loose Wires

    You turned a flat ribbon into three harnesses that each carry their own ground and plug in as blocks. Now a module comes off and goes back on in seconds, and the wires stay in order every time.
