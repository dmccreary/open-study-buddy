    Lab 05: Speaker Tones¶

    Give Your Buddy a Voice

    Today your Study Buddy learns to make sound, starting with a plain beep and ending with a scale you can sing along to. The Pico cannot hear whether this works, so you are the test equipment. Let's listen closely!
    Program 05-speaker-tones.py
    Parts needed The MAX98357A amplifier and the speaker
    Time 15 minutes
    Makes sound? Yes. Keep the speaker a little away from your ear.
    How to stop The program ends by itself after about 6 seconds

    What You Will Learn¶

    • How the Pico turns a list of numbers into sound
    • What I2S is and which pins carry it
    • How to build a sine wave with math.sin
    • Why a short fade at each end of a note stops it from clicking
    • What a decibel step is, and why doubling the numbers does not sound twice as loud

    Wiring¶

    Amplifier pin Pico pin What it does
    BCLK GP9 The "bit clock": one tick for every bit of audio
    LRC GP10 The "left/right clock": says which channel a sample belongs to
    DIN GP11 The audio data itself
    GAIN GP12 Chooses how loud the amplifier makes the signal
    VIN 5 V (VBUS) or 3V3 Power
    GND GND Ground

    Connect the speaker to the amplifier's two output terminals (+ and -).

    The I2S Pin Rule

    MicroPython's I2S on the Pico needs LRC on the pin right after BCLK, which is why this kit uses GP9 and GP10. If you swap them, I2S() raises an error, and the fix is to put LRC back on the next pin up from BCLK.

    The Big Idea¶

    A speaker makes sound by moving a cone in and out. The Pico cannot move the cone smoothly. It sends a list of numbers, and each number says where the cone should be at one instant. Played fast enough, the numbers make a smooth wave. Each number is called a sample.

    This lab sends 16,000 samples every second (the sample rate). Each sample is a 16-bit number from -32768 to 32767. Positive pushes the cone out, negative pulls it in, and zero is the middle. The shape of the list decides the sound, and the speed of the wiggle decides the pitch:

    Frequency Musical meaning
    220 Hz A3
    440 Hz A4, the "tuning A"
    880 Hz A5, one octave above

    Every time the frequency doubles, the note goes up one octave.

    What is I2S?¶

    I2S (Inter-IC Sound) is a way for chips to send digital audio over three wires. It is like SPI, but made for sound:

    I2S wire Job
    Bit clock (BCLK) Ticks once for every bit
    Word select (LRC) Flips between left and right channel
    Data (DIN) The bits of each sample

    The amplifier receives the samples, converts them into a smooth voltage, and boosts that voltage enough to drive the speaker. You do not need a DAC or any resistors.

    Walking Through the Code¶

    Making a note¶

    def make_tone(freq, ms, volume=config.VOLUME):
    count = RATE * ms // 1000
    amplitude = 32767 * volume / 100
    out = bytearray(count * 2)
    for i in range(count):
        fade = min(1.0, i / FADE, (count - i) / FADE)
        value = int(amplitude * fade * math.sin(2 * math.pi * freq * i / RATE))
        struct.pack_into("<h", out, i * 2, value)
    return out

    Take it one line at a time:

    • count = RATE * ms // 1000 is how many samples a note of ms milliseconds needs. For an 800 ms beep: 16000 x 800 / 1000 = 12,800 samples.
    • amplitude is the biggest value the wave will reach. At 100% volume it is 32767. The kit's VOLUME setting is 40, so the amplitude is about 13,000.
    • bytearray(count * 2) makes room for the samples. Each sample is 2 bytes (16 bits).
    • math.sin(2 * math.pi * freq * i / RATE) is the heart of it. For sample number i, the time is i / RATE seconds. A wave of freq cycles per second has traveled freq * time cycles by then, and a full cycle is 2 * pi radians. The sine of that angle is a number between -1 and 1.
    • fade smoothly goes from 0 up to 1 over the first 10 ms and back to 0 over the last 10 ms. Without it, a note that starts or stops in the middle of a wave makes the cone jump, and you hear a click.
    • struct.pack_into("<h", out, i * 2, value) writes the number into the array as a 16-bit signed integer (h), least significant byte first (<).

    A Sound Is Just a List

    There is no "sound" object anywhere in this program, only a list of numbers made by a formula. Change the formula and you change the sound, which is why every later audio lab in this kit is a new way of filling a list.

    Starting I2S¶

    config.set_gain()
audio = I2S(config.I2S_SPEAKER_ID,
            sck=Pin(config.I2S_BCLK_PIN), ws=Pin(config.I2S_LRC_PIN),
            sd=Pin(config.I2S_DIN_PIN), mode=I2S.TX, bits=16,
            format=I2S.MONO, rate=RATE, ibuf=IBUF)
    • set_gain() sets the amplifier's loudness (9 dB by default).
    • mode=I2S.TX means "transmit" (the Pico sends sound out).
    • bits=16 and format=I2S.MONO say each sample is 16 bits and there is one channel.
    • ibuf=8192 is the size of a buffer that holds up to 8192 bytes, which is 256 ms of sound at this sample rate. The Pico fills the buffer, and I2S empties it at exactly the right speed.

    Playing the sound¶

    audio.write(make_tone(440, 800))
audio.write(silence(300))

    audio.write() hands the samples to the buffer. When the buffer is full, write waits until there is room. The program ends with a short sleep and audio.deinit(), so the last bytes finish playing before the speaker is released.

    Part 2: the scale¶

    The scale is eight notes, C4 up to C5. The program builds every note first, and then plays them. If it built each note right before playing it, the speaker would run out of numbers while the Pico was busy calculating, and you would hear gaps.

    Note C4 D4 E4 F4 G4 A4 B4 C5
    Hz 262 294 330 349 392 440 494 523

    Notice that C5 (523 Hz) is almost exactly double C4 (262 Hz): an octave.

    Part 3: three volumes¶

    for percent in (10, 30, 100):
    level = config.VOLUME * percent // 100
    audio.write(make_tone(440, 400, level) + silence(125))

    The program plays the same note at 10%, 30%, and 100% of VOLUME. Each step is about 10 decibels louder, which is the step your ears hear as "twice as loud". Doubling the numbers (25%, 50%, 100%) is only 6 dB per step, and on a small speaker that sounds almost the same.

    Run It¶

    Open 05-speaker-tones.py in Thonny and press Run. Keep the speaker at arm's length: a speaker an inch from your ear is much louder than you expect.

    What PASS Looks Like¶

    You hear three things in order:

    1. A steady beep (A4, 440 Hz)
    2. A rising scale of eight notes
    3. Three beeps that get louder each time

    The shell prints each note name and frequency as it plays, and ends with Done. Did you hear all three parts?

    If Something Goes Wrong¶

    What you hear or see Likely cause Fix
    Silence Amplifier power or speaker wires Check VIN, GND, and the speaker + and - terminals
    Silence, but no error Signal wires Check BCLK (GP9), LRC (GP10), DIN (GP11)
    ValueError from I2S() LRC is not right after BCLK Check the pins in config.py
    Buzzing or crackling at full volume The speaker is running out of room to move Lower VOLUME in config.py
    A quiet hiss Normal at low volume Ignore it; it is the amplifier

    Try This¶

    1. Octaves. Change 440 in part 1 to 220, then to 880. What happens to the pitch each time you double the frequency?
    2. Your own song. Write a list of (name, frequency) pairs and play them the way part 2 does. "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star" starts C C G G A A G.
    3. More gain. Change GAIN_DB from 9 to 12 in config.py, upload it, and run again. How much louder is it? Start with a low VOLUME.
    4. A siren. Play 600 Hz for 300 ms, then 900 Hz for 300 ms, and repeat. How does it sound?
    Hint for number 2

    Use a dictionary to look up frequencies by name: {"C": 262, "D": 294, "E": 330, "F": 349, "G": 392, "A": 440}. Then a song is a string like "CCGGAAG", and you loop over the letters. Dictionaries are in the Collections chapter of the course.

    Check Your Understanding¶

    1. How many samples are in a 250 ms note at a 16,000 Hz sample rate?
    2. Why does each note start and end with a 10 ms fade?
    3. What is the frequency of the note one octave above 330 Hz?
    Answers
    1. 16000 x 250 / 1000 = 4,000 samples.
    2. To keep the cone from jumping at the start and end of the note, which would make a click.
    3. 660 Hz. An octave up doubles the frequency.
