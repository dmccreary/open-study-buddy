    Lab 04: Hello World on the Display¶

    Time to Give Your Buddy a Face

    Up to now your Study Buddy could only talk through the shell. In this lab it says "Hello World!" on a round color screen, and everything you build from here on will show up there. Let's listen closely!
    Program 04-hello-world.py
    Parts needed The display
    Time 10 minutes
    Makes sound? No
    How to stop The program ends by itself after drawing

    What You Will Learn¶

    • How to start the display with one line, config.init_display()
    • How the screen's x and y coordinates work
    • Why the round screen has a safe radius
    • How to center text, using the width of a font

    Wiring¶

    The display uses the Pico's first SPI bus (SPI0). You learned SPI in the Communication Protocols chapter, and the display is the same idea as the SPI OLED screens there, only bigger and in color.

    Display pin Pico pin Wire color in the kit What it does
    GND GND black Ground
    VCC 3V3 red Power
    SCL GP2 orange SPI clock
    SDA GP3 yellow SPI data (the pixels)
    RST GP4 green Reset
    DC GP5 blue Says "this byte is a command" or "this byte is a pixel"
    CS GP6 purple Chip select
    BL GP7 gray Backlight

    The module also has SDO and TE pads. Leave them unconnected: the driver never uses them.

    The Big Idea¶

    The display is a grid of 360 x 360 pixels. Every pixel has an x (how far from the left) and a y (how far from the top). The point (0, 0) is the top-left, and y grows downward, which surprises people who remember graphs from math class.

    (0,0) ───────────► x
  │
  │
  ▼
  y                         (359, 359)

    But the glass is round. Only the circle drawn inside the square is visible, and the four corners of the square are hidden under the bezel. Drawing there wastes time sending pixels that nobody will ever see. config.py stores the geometry for you:

    Name in config.py Value Meaning
    WIDTH, HEIGHT 360 The size of the square
    CENTER_X, CENTER_Y 180 The middle of the screen
    RADIUS 180 The edge of the glass
    SAFE_RADIUS 168 The biggest circle you can draw without the bezel clipping it

    The Program¶

    import config
import shapes

display = config.init_display()
config.set_backlight(True)

display.fill(config.BLACK)

shapes.ring(display, config.CENTER_X, config.CENTER_Y, config.SAFE_RADIUS,
            config.CYAN, 3)

config.centered_text(display, config.BIG_FONT, "Hello World!",
                     config.CENTER_Y - 16, config.YELLOW)
config.centered_text(display, config.SMALL_FONT, "Study Buddy is alive",
                     config.CENTER_Y + 30, config.WHITE)

print("Drawn: look at the screen.")

    Walking Through the Code¶

    import config and import shapes. config holds every pin and color for the kit, and shapes is a small library (in lib/shapes.py) that draws circles, rings, and polygons.

    display = config.init_display() does a lot of work in one line. It starts the SPI bus at 24 MHz, sets up the reset, DC, chip-select, and backlight pins, and builds the display object that the driver provides. Look at init_display() in config.py to see everything it does. Putting this in config.py means no lab has to repeat it.

    config.set_backlight(True) turns the backlight on. Without it, the display is drawing but you cannot see anything.

    display.fill(config.BLACK) paints every pixel black. Colors such as BLACK, CYAN, and YELLOW are numbers stored in config.py. Lab 08 explains how a color is a number.

    shapes.ring(display, x, y, radius, color, thickness) draws a circle outline. Here it is centered on the screen, with a radius of 168, in cyan, 3 pixels thick. That puts the ring right at the visible edge of the glass.

    config.centered_text(...) draws text so that it is centered left to right. The big font is 16 pixels wide per letter, so "Hello World!" (12 letters) is 12 x 16 = 192 pixels wide. To center it, the function computes the left edge:

    x = (WIDTH - len(text) * font.WIDTH) // 2

    That is (360 - 192) / 2 = 84 pixels from the left. The // means whole-number division.

    Centering Anything

    To center anything, subtract its width from the screen's width and divide by two. You will use that same recipe for text, bars, and pictures in every lab from here on.

    The y values. CENTER_Y - 16 places the top of the big letters 16 pixels above the middle. The big letters are 32 pixels tall, so half of the height (16) sits above the center line and half below. The small caption goes at CENTER_Y + 30, just under the big text.

    Run It¶

    Open 04-hello-world.py and press Run. The Pico draws and the program ends. The picture stays on the screen, because the display remembers what was last drawn until you draw something else.

    What PASS Looks Like¶

    The screen goes black, then shows a cyan ring, Hello World! in big yellow letters, and the small caption "Study Buddy is alive" underneath. All the letters are inside the ring, and none are cut off.

    If Something Goes Wrong¶

    What you see Likely cause Fix
    Completely dark Backlight or power Check BL (GP7) and the VCC and GND wires
    Bright white screen or random noise SPI or control wires Check SCL (GP2), SDA (GP3), RST (GP4), DC (GP5), CS (GP6)
    Picture works but is speckled or torn Wires too long or loose Use short wires, or set BAUDRATE = 12_000_000 in config.py
    ImportError: no module named 'gc9b72' The lib/ folder is not on the Pico Run ./upload-code.sh
    Text cut off at the sides Text too long Shorten it, or use the small font

    Blank Screen, But Lab 02 Said All OK?

    Lab 02 can feel the SCL, SDA, and BL wires but not RST, DC, or CS, because those three have no resistor to sense. If the probe is clean and the screen is blank, check RST on GP4, DC on GP5, and CS on GP6 by eye.

    Try This¶

    1. Your name. Change "Hello World!" to your name. How long can a name be before it runs off the ring?
    2. Small font. Swap BIG_FONT for SMALL_FONT. The small font is 8 pixels wide, so how many more letters fit across the screen?
    3. A new color. Replace config.YELLOW with config.color565(255, 128, 0) for orange. (Lab 08 shows how this function works.)
    4. A second ring. Add another ring with shapes.ring(...) at a smaller radius, such as 140, in magenta. Try three rings in a row.
    5. Off center. What happens if you change config.CENTER_X to 0 in a call to shapes.ring? Which parts of the ring do you lose?
    Hint for number 2

    The screen is 360 pixels wide. Big letters are 16 wide, so 360 / 16 = 22 letters. Small letters are 8 wide, so 360 / 8 = 45. But the round edge cuts off the ends of a long line, so the safe number is smaller near the top and bottom.

    Check Your Understanding¶

    1. Which corner of the screen is (0, 0), and which way does y grow?
    2. Why is SAFE_RADIUS smaller than RADIUS?
    3. How wide, in pixels, is "Study Buddy is alive" in the small font?
    Answers
    1. The top-left corner. y grows downward.
    2. The bezel (the plastic edge) covers the outermost pixels of the glass. A ring at the full radius would be partly hidden.
    3. 20 letters x 8 pixels = 160 pixels.

    Your Buddy Has a Face

    You just started an SPI display, found the center of a round screen, and centered text by computing its width. That is the foundation for every picture in the rest of the kit.
